# Data

All raw data are regenerated by `experiments/01_data.py` into `data/raw/` (git-ignored).
`data/raw/manifest.json` (committed) records the SHA-256, size, and download time of the
exact files used for the paper's results.

## Sources (accessed 2026-10-05)

| File | Source | Content |
|---|---|---|
| `sp500_membership.csv` | [fja05680/sp500](https://github.com/fja05680/sp500), `S&P 500 Historical Components & Changes (Updated).csv` | Dated snapshots of S&P 500 constituents, 1996-01-02 → 2026-08-18 (2,720 snapshots). Only snapshots ≤ 2025-12-31 are used. |
| `prices_raw.parquet` | Yahoo Finance via `yfinance` 1.7.0, `auto_adjust=True` | Daily split- and dividend-adjusted closes, 1999-01-01 → 2025-12-31, for every ticker that was ever a member in the sample window, plus SPY and ^VIX. |
| `market.parquet` | same | SPY (benchmark) and ^VIX (regimes). |
| `usrec.csv` | FRED series `USREC` | NBER recession indicator (monthly). |
| `sectors_current.csv` | Wikipedia "List of S&P 500 companies" | **Current** GICS sector per ticker. |
| `prices_clean.parquet` | derived | Cleaned panel used by every experiment. |

Adjusted close is used as the cumulative total-return index of GGR (dividends reinvested).

## Cleaning pipeline (in order)

1. **Ticker mapping.** `BRK.B` → `BRK-B` (Yahoo convention).
2. **Cumulative download.** Yahoo intermittently drops tickers under rate limiting, so the
   raw cache is a union over attempts; missing tickers are retried in batches of 200, 50, 10.
   Repeated runs converged at **691 of 1,117** tickers. The other 426 tickers return no
   data at all: Yahoo deletes the history of delisted securities (e.g. companies acquired
   or taken private), including several 2025–26 delistings such as EA, HOLX, DAY.
3. **Membership-spell masking.** For each ticker, prices are kept only from the start of
   each index-membership spell to 18 months after it ends (`data.mask_outside_spells`).
   Rationale: tickers are reused (the `AAL` that was in the index in 1996 is not American
   Airlines Group). A portfolio formed while a stock is a member needs at most
   12 + 6 = 18 months of data after the formation start, so the mask never removes a
   price that any portfolio can use, but it does remove history belonging to an
   unrelated security that later took the ticker.
4. **Non-positive prices → missing; interior gaps ≤ 5 days forward-filled.** Gaps after a
   stock's last price are never filled (a delisting must not create fake zero returns).
5. **Stale / reused-ticker screen.** Tickers with > 10% zero daily returns are dropped
   (`data.drop_stale`): `AIT, CSR, EP, MMI, PARA, SCI, SII, SNT, CPWR, GR, SBNY`. Index
   members trade every day; long runs of unchanged prices indicate Yahoo is serving an
   illiquid OTC security under a reused symbol (e.g. `CPWR` shows +375% days that are not
   Compuware). This is the closest available proxy for GGR's "no days without trades"
   screen. Genuine crashes (FNMA, FMCC, AIG, GNW, EHC/HealthSouth 2003) are kept.
6. **Formation screen (in the engine).** On each formation date the universe is the
   point-in-time members with no missing price in the 12-month formation window.

After cleaning, **618 tickers** carry data.

## Coverage — the residual survivorship bias

`tables/data_coverage.csv`, `figures/fig01_coverage.png`. Share of 1 January index
members with a complete price history in that year:

| Year | 1999 | 2000 | 2005 | 2010 | 2013 | 2015 | 2020 | 2025 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Members | 493 | 492 | 495 | 499 | 497 | 499 | 505 | 503 |
| With complete prices | 215 | 226 | 259 | 317 | 346 | 360 | 435 | 486 |
| Share | 44% | 46% | 52% | 64% | 70% | 72% | 86% | 97% |

Every year is in the CSV.

**Interpretation.** The missing names are not random: they are disproportionately firms
that were later acquired, merged, went bankrupt, or were taken private. Excluding them
removes exactly the pairs in which one leg experiences a takeover jump or a collapse —
events that typically *hurt* a convergence trade. The likely direction of the bias is
therefore **upward** on pairs-trading returns, and it is strongest in the early years
(the development sample, 2000–2012). The holdout (2013–2025) is much better covered
(70–97%). The paper reports results by sub-period for this reason. Fixing this fully
requires a survivorship-free source (CRSP, or a vendor with delisted tickers such as
Tiingo/EODHD/Norgate).

## Other known biases and caveats

- **Sectors are not point-in-time.** The Wikipedia list covers current constituents
  only, so sector-restricted pairs are formed only among stocks that are still members
  today (an additional survivorship filter on that one variant: 79% of the 2000 formation
  universe has a sector, 92% in 2024) and use today's GICS classification. The sector
  portfolio therefore uses future information and is reported for comparison only.
- **Adjusted close ≈ total return.** Yahoo's dividend adjustment is multiplicative and
  occasionally contains errors; 50 daily returns exceed |50%| after cleaning, almost all
  attributable to real events (2008 financials, 2003 HealthSouth).
- **Yahoo data are revised and pruned over time.** Re-downloading later will return
  fewer delisted tickers. The manifest's hashes identify the exact files used. Yahoo's
  terms do not permit redistributing the price files, so they are not included in the
  repository or the Zenodo archive.
